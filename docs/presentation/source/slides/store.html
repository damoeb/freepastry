USE CASE · STORING AND FINDING AN OBJECT

Past keeps copies next to the object’s id

  1. The object’s id is the SHA-256 hash of its content (in Glacier: of the owner’s key and the object’s name).
  2. The insert is routed to the root of that id.
  3. The root and its nearest leaf-set neighbours store replicas and acknowledge; the insert succeeds once enough of them have.
  4. A lookup is routed towards the id and answered by the first replica it meets.
  5. The reader checks the hash and rejects a wrong answer.

Past is the storage layer on top of Pastry; ExpiringPast adds leases capped at a year, and Glacier adds erasure-coded, owner-signed archives. Because replicas sit on the leaf set of the root, the set heals itself when nodes come and go: replication moves copies to the new neighbours.
